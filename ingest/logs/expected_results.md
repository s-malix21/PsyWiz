## Association between household dietary diversity and depression symptoms:
Higher household dietary diversity was associated with **lower symptoms of depression** in both boys and girls. The incident rate ratios (IRRs) were 0.906 for boys and 0.919 for girls, indicating that increased dietary diversity reduces depression symptoms.

## Maternal mental health mediation:
Maternal mental health **significantly mediated** the relationship between food insecurity and adolescent mental health. The structural equation models showed that food insecurity negatively affected maternal mental well-being (β = -0.840 in boys, β = -0.704 in girls), which in turn was associated with increased depression and anxiety symptoms in adolescents.

## Incident rate ratios for food insecurity:
- **Boys**: Food insecurity was associated with depression symptoms (IRR = 1.52, CI: 1.17-1.99) and anxiety symptoms (IRR = 1.33, CI: 1.14-1.55)
- **Girls**: Food insecurity was associated with anxiety symptoms (IRR = 1.22, CI: 1.07-1.39) and potentially with depression symptoms (IRR = 1.26, CI: 1.00-1.60)

## Instruments used for measuring mental health:
- **Depression**: Short Mood and Feelings Questionnaire (SMFQ) - 13 items, score range 0-26, cut-off >8 for significant symptoms
- **Anxiety**: Screen for Child Anxiety-related Disorders (SCARED) - modified to 37 items, score range 0-74 (school avoidance subscale excluded)

## Folate deficiency prevalence rates:
- **Boys**: 72.1% had folate deficiency (37.3% deficient + 34.8% possible deficiency)
- **Girls**: 64.0% had folate deficiency (26.0% deficient + 38.0% possible deficiency)

Normal folate levels were found in only 14.8% of boys and 17.7% of girls.
